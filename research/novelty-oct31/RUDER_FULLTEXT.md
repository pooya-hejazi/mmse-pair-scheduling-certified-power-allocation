**Conclusion:** the 2014 paper resolves the earlier receiver uncertainty and is closer prior art than the initial screen could establish. It uses exactly two single-antenna users, linear-MMSE post-equalization log rates, frequency-selective pair allocation, and an integer packing formulation. Under a flat-within-resource specialization its rate reduces to our fixed-power pair rate. The three papers do not supply our rational near-orthogonal realization, LP four-cycle witness, or continuous-power certificate. That absence in these sources is not evidence of originality. Their hardness assertions invoke general assignment hardness rather than proving the restricted rational-channel theorem. Their polynomial algorithms do not assert general exactness of the joint frequency-selective scheduling LP.

## Actual models and objectives

**R11:** Single-antenna users transmit equal power with the same modulation/coding on equally sized, contiguous frequency chunks; all users are paired and allocated once. Channels are frequency-selective, fixed over a subframe, perfectly known at the receiver, with spatially/temporally white Gaussian noise (PDF pp.1-3, Secs.II-IV). Receivers considered in simulation are linear MMSE and SIC with decoded feedback. However, the scheduling score is the average per-subcarrier Gaussian log-determinant capacity, Eq.(13), summed across pairs in Eq.(14), not the sum of individual interference-treated-as-noise linear-MMSE log rates. BER/BLER is the evaluated communication outcome. Fixed power is explicit at the start of Sec.IV, PDF p.3; power/rate allocation is future work in Sec.VI, PDF p.6.

**R13:** The journal treatment covers two-user TDMA pairing and FDMA grouping with group size Nu, including Nu=2; multiple receive antennas, perfect CSI, equal powers, common modulation/coding, and equally sized chunks remain (PDF pp.2-5, printed pp.92-95). TDMA channels are fixed over the entire block of user-pair transmissions; FDMA channels need only be fixed for one subframe. The MMSE error variance is the frequency average of diagonal error-covariance terms, Eqs.(13)-(15), p.94. SIC is also described. Its two scheduling criteria are Gaussian logdet capacity, Eqs.(28)-(30), p.95, and average approximate BER based on post-equalizer unbiased SINR, Eqs.(31)-(38), pp.95-96. The paper explicitly calls Gaussian capacity an upper bound on QAM mutual information, and BER is an approximation involving a coding-gain parameter. Neither criterion is our unweighted sum of two post-MMSE log rates. Fixed power and rate remain assumptions, explicitly left for future relaxation in the conclusion (PDF p.11/p.101).

**R14:** Exactly two users per pair, one transmit antenna each, multiple receive antennas, AWGN, and contiguous resource patterns (RPs), including all adjacent-RB intervals. There are NRB(NRB+1)/2 RPs and K(K-1)/2 possible pairs (PDF p.2/p.89, Sec.2.1). The signal is y = H sqrt(P) s + n, Eq.(1); P is subsequently fixed to the identity, with pathloss/large-scale fading ideally compensated (PDF p.3/p.90). Although a power matrix appears in the equations, there is no joint continuous-power optimization.

R14's user rate is BSC times the number of allocated subcarriers times log2(1+gamma), Eq.(3). For the SC-FDMA block, gamma is the reciprocal of the average diagonal MMSE error term minus one, Eqs.(4)-(6), PDF pp.3-4/printed pp.90-91. This is a post-linear-MMSE rate, not logdet capacity or SIC. It averages MSE across the assigned subcarriers before applying the reciprocal/log; it is not generally the average of per-subcarrier log rates.

For a constant channel across the subcarriers of a resource, noise variance one, and P=I, Eq.(6) becomes gamma_u = 1/[(I+H^H H)^(-1)]_uu - 1. Inverting the two-by-two matrix gives gamma_u = g_u - |h_u^H h_v|^2/(1+g_v), exactly the proof note's identity. The common bandwidth factor cancels from optimization when resource sizes agree. Equal historical-rate weights in R14's Eq.(26) reduce its utility to a common multiple of the same pair sum rate. **This algebraic specialization is our inference from their equations, not a theorem stated in R14.** Variable-size SC-FDMA RPs and unequal fairness weights prevent identifying the full models without those restrictions.

R14 estimates scheduling CSI by LS pilots followed by frequency interpolation and time prediction; channel correlation follows WSSUS/Jakes assumptions (Secs.3.1-3.3, pp.91-93). Crucially, its simulations schedule on estimated/predicted CSI but evaluate on true CSI and explicitly assume perfect CSI for payload detection/equalization, to isolate scheduling effects (Sec.5, PDF p.9/p.96). SRS overhead is included. Thus the project cannot claim novelty for the methodological separation of scheduling-CSI benefits from receiver-estimation benefits.

## Integer formulation, exactness, and complexity

**R11/R13 frequency scheduling:** Assign every user to one pair/group and every group to one equal-sized disjoint chunk. R11 PDF p.3/Sec.IV-A and R13 PDF p.7/p.97/Sec.6.1 call this a three-dimensional assignment problem, NP-hard in general, citing the assignment-problems textbook. They do not give a reduction from a discrete NP-complete problem through physical channel coefficients, a rational encoding argument, or a near-orthogonality restriction. The generic assignment observation supports broad prior art, but by itself does not prove hardness for every channel-realizable weight subclass.

No configuration LP, LP integrality theorem, or LP dual certificate is developed in R11/R13. The exact benchmark is full enumeration: Nuser!/(Nu!)^(Nuser/Nu), R13 Eq.(41), or Nuser!/2^(Nuser/2), R11 Eq.(16). Hungarian assignment is exact **for fixed groups assigned to chunks**: R11 PDF p.4/Sec.IV-C; R13 PDF p.7/p.97/Sec.6.3. Changing group members uses binary switching. Alternation is explicitly suboptimal in general, with monotone objective improvement but no global-optimality guarantee or general certified gap (R11 pp.4-5; R13 p.98/Sec.6.5).

Both papers identify a special four-user/two-pair case where one HA+BSA iteration examines all six integral pair/chunk schedules, hence finds the integral optimum (R11 PDF pp.4-5/Fig.5; R13 PDF p.8/p.98/Fig.5). This says nothing about equality between the integer optimum and a fractional LP optimum. Our four-user gap and this tiny-instance exact enumeration can both hold.

**R13 time-only exactness:** Resource-independent pairing is weighted perfect matching on a non-bipartite graph. PDF p.6/p.96/Sec.5 explicitly warns that Hungarian assignment with a symmetric weight matrix need not return a symmetric pairing; it refers to additional processing and gives blossom matching as an alternative with cubic complexity. This is a useful correction to any claim that an ordinary unconstrained symmetric assignment automatically solves pairing. The exact polynomial subclass depends on time-independent pair weights; it does not extend directly to independently resource-dependent pair choices. The paper does not establish a polynomial worst-case bound for the referenced enumeration of alternative symmetric assignments; its blossom alternative avoids relying on that route.

**Coefficient evaluation matters:** The cubic HA/blossom statements are combinatorial operation counts with supplied edge weights. R11 Eq.(18), PDF p.5, counts Nit(5 Nuser^2/4 - 2 Nuser) capacity evaluations; R13 Eq.(43), PDF p.8/p.98, generalizes this to Nit[(1+1/Nu^2)Nuser^2 - Nu Nuser]. These are quadratic *metric-evaluation counts* for fixed iteration budget, explicitly neglecting HA's arithmetic relative to metric costs. Each metric itself requires channel/frequency operations, matrix computations, and log or Q-function evaluation. These equations are not total quadratic bit-complexity theorems, nor exact real-number comparison analyses. An arbitrary run until convergence is not given a polynomial iteration bound. The proof note's rational threshold encoding addresses a different formal issue.

**R14 integer packing:** At a given subframe, historical rates are fixed coefficients. Eq.(26), PDF p.7/p.94, is the **sum** of the two rates divided by their own exponentially averaged historical rates, not a product. Eq.(27) maximizes the sum of pair/RP utilities times binary indicators. Eq.(28a-c) imposes at-most-one constraints for pairs, RPs, and users. Eq.(28d) excludes overlapping selected RPs. Users may pause; the footnote explains that the history-weighted utility balances this over time. This is not the proof note's all-users/all-resources equality model, though both share pair/resource conflict structure.

R14 describes Eq.(27)-(28) as NP-hard integer optimization, supplies full enumeration as the joint-optimal benchmark, and summarizes HA+BSA, FAN, and KIM heuristics (PDF pp.7-8/pp.94-95). It does not formulate or prove exactness of an LP relaxation or give a dual-feasibility certificate. The perfect-CSI full-search curve is the paper's numerical upper-performance reference for its small scenario (PDF p.9/p.96), not a continuous-power upper bound. General RP overlap would require additional linear conflict constraints if writing an explicit LP; simply relaxing x in the displayed logical condition (28d) is not a complete LP definition.
