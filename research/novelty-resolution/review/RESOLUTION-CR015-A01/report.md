## R1 — Claims, source assumptions, and hypotheses

The fixed-dimensional reduction concerns a complete eligible-pair family, exactly one pair per resource, every user served exactly once, identity noise, and true-channel linear MMSE with interference treated as noise. It is not joint-decoding log-det capacity or SIC. The construction permits independently chosen resource-specific channel vectors. The stated result is M=3 sufficiency, rational unit-norm entries from a fixed finite alphabet, fixed-power ideal-threshold NP-completeness, and an instance-size-independent additive gap. Its positive-box extension claims hardness and a constant gap, without claiming general continuous-threshold NP membership.

The external combinatorial premise is **perfect** 3DM with every element appearing at most three times. The packet identifies an original-author theorem statement with a page and equation reference; it does not incorrectly substitute maximum-cardinality approximation hardness for the perfect decision problem. The stronger cited two-or-three-occurrence restriction would imply the needed restriction. The internal reduction is valid on that premise. The cited PDF and theorem source were not independently fetched or frozen here, so this audit does not certify the bibliography's exact quotations, pages, or source interpretation. The rational representation is proved directly; its correctness does not rely on accepting the cited geometric ancestry as an oracle.

The continuous claims use an unweighted two-user, common-receiver MMSE sum rate, normalized positive gains and squared correlation r in [0,1]. Prices can be signed. Independent compact rectangles, rather than rectangles intersected with an undealt-with local sum constraint, are essential to the 17-candidate theorem. Zero gains, degenerate rectangles, r=0, log-base conversion, and physical-price scaling are explicitly separated. No claim covers unequal rate weights, extra interferers, QoS, or arbitrary uncertainty.

## R2 — Proof, encoding, and edge cases

**Rational forest representation: PASS.** The root parametrization has unit norm and the nonzero dot polynomial has degree at most four. The parallelism polynomial has degree at most eight and cannot vanish identically. Six earlier vectors therefore exclude at most 72 parameters, below the 101 offered integers. The child Householder formula is rational and well-defined except at the explicitly treated p=e3 case; p=-e3 is harmless. Nonparallel parent/earlier vectors prevent an identically zero child dot polynomial. Parent-before-child traversal makes the parent the sole earlier neighbor. The finite number of canonically relabelled templates and bounded rational operations justify constant bit length without numerical enumeration.

**Reduction and gaps: PASS.** At most three distinct bipartite edges form a forest. Its six possible active endpoints plus the isolated outsider fit the lemma. Reusing the outsider vector gives dot product one between outsiders, so it creates no spurious ideal pair. All nonzero relevant dot products belong to a fixed finite rational set; its minimum is positive. The MMSE fixed-power rate and product threshold are correct. Products of K constant-size rational factors and the threshold 2^K have polynomial encoding length. A schedule attaining the ideal threshold selects exactly a perfect 3DM. The result is constant **additive** separation, not constant-relative approximation hardness. Under a strictly positive box, Jensen and the uniform lower bound on p/(1+p) correctly give the stated joint-power gap. The p_min=0 case is correctly excluded from that gap formula.

**Sharp global concavity and box characterization: PASS.** Necessity follows from the limiting positive second derivative and continuity into the interior. I independently differentiated the objective and expanded the determinant: all 25 coefficients agree with the table. The supplied coefficient bounds are valid for a in [1/sqrt(2),1], with a positive constant term; the threshold equality therefore retains negative definiteness at finite points. The cross-corner diagonal tests use the correct monotonicities. The determinant must still be nonnegative throughout the box; the text correctly avoids claiming an unsupported four-corner rule. Lower-dimensional boxes require only their restricted curvature.

**Finite candidate theorem: PASS as an exact mathematical characterization.** The independent symbolic calculation reproduced both stationarity equations, the corrected quintic, its degree bound, and P(-1)=2r^3. The B=0 case cannot contain an interior stationary point because simultaneous C=0 would imply B=-2r. This prevents a lost branch. The edge polynomials cannot be identically zero. Corners, edge stationary points, and interior stationary points exhaust maximizers on compact rectangles. Repeated roots, zero or signed prices, and r=1 do not invalidate the count. The r=0 separable solution and collapsed rectangles are handled separately. Candidate coordinates are algebraic for algebraic inputs; exact comparison of their transcendental objective values is not assumed to terminate.

**Restricted simplex envelope: PASS.** I checked the undivided identity comparing d^2 with (1+x+y)(1+x)(1+y), preserving axis cases.
