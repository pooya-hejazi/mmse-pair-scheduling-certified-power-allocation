## R1 — Contract and complete candidate coverage

The implementation matches the corrected Theorem 2 objective and elimination polynomial: unweighted true-channel two-user MMSE, normalized squared correlation in [0,1], signed prices, and compact nonnegative rectangular powers. Rational conversion determines the exact model being certified. In particular, converting a binary float to Fraction certifies that represented rational, not an intended irrational physical parameter or its uncertainty.

For positive correlation, every interior stationary point has B nonzero: C=0 at a feasible x forces B=-2r. Removing all common polynomial factors of P and B therefore removes only roots that cannot be feasible interior stationary points. The repeated-gcd loop removes their full multiplicity, and square-free reduction preserves every remaining distinct root. P cannot be identically zero because P(-1)=2r^3. B cannot be the zero polynomial in the positive-correlation domain. Exact rational real-root isolation avoids imaginary-part cutoffs and floating-root loss.

Clipping each isolating x interval against the domain cannot discard a feasible root. Interval evaluation of -C/B encloses its recovered y whenever the denominator interval excludes zero; clipping that y interval also retains every feasible stationary point. False-positive overlapping intervals can add candidates but cannot invalidate an upper bound. An unresolved denominator interval causes refinement of the entire computation, not silent candidate removal.

All four corners and roots of every horizontal and vertical edge polynomial are included. Edge polynomials are nonzero even at zero prices, so there is no missed stationary continuum. Collapsed rectangles retain the appropriate one-dimensional edge search; singleton rectangles retain their point. At r=0 the separate scalar clipped optimizer is correct for positive, zero, and negative prices. Boundary duplicates and extraneous candidate boxes are harmless. Coupled local constraints, unequal rate weights, extra interferers, and arbitrary irrational input are outside this acceptance.

## R2 — Arithmetic direction and failure correctness

Fraction interval addition, multiplication, negation, subtraction, division with a zero-excluding denominator, and Horner polynomial evaluation preserve inclusion. The reciprocal endpoints are ordered correctly for either denominator sign. Feasible candidate boxes have nonnegative coordinates and positive logarithm arguments.

For each positive rational log argument, directed Decimal division first encloses the argument. Correctly rounded Decimal.ln followed by neighboring representable numbers encloses the logarithm at either rounded endpoint; monotonicity then encloses the exact rational logarithm. The special endpoint equal to one contributes the exact value zero and preserves direction. Combining these bounds with Fraction arithmetic avoids further floating-point rounding. This acceptance explicitly trusts the stated SymPy root-isolation and Decimal logarithm contracts; those libraries were not independently implemented or formally verified here.

The maximum of all candidate-box upper bounds bounds the true optimum because at least one box contains a maximizer. Every returned lower-bound midpoint lies in the rectangle; its outward lower objective bound is therefore attainable from below. Midpoints need not be stationary. The fixed 12/24/36-digit root-refinement sequence can fail on poorly separated roots or requested gaps below the fixed logarithm precision. On exhaustion it raises ArithmeticError instead of returning a fabricated enclosure. Arithmetic/resource exceptions likewise do not return a successful certificate. The function itself has no wall-clock guard; the supplied external worker wrapper supplies that separate execution limit.

Independent checks completed successfully in 0.909 seconds of externally bounded worker time, against a 30-second limit. Eleven cases cover exact denominator-factor cancellation, a symbolically verified repeated root at a boundary, signed prices, r=1, r=0, r=10^-30, positive lower bounds, narrow boxes, collapsed axes, and a singleton. Sampling is corroboration, not the completeness proof above.
